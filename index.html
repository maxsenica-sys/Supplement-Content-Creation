Research Standards STUDY QUALITY

PubMed is global — indexes AIS (Australia), UK, Europe, Japan, Brazil, Scandinavia, USA. Best study wins regardless of country.

Mandatory quality requirements

  • RCT or meta-analysis of RCTs — gold standard only
  • Human subjects only — no animal or in-vitro
  • Min 15 participants
  • Quantified outcomes — specific %, kg, seconds, biomarker values
  • Real PMID or DOI — independently verifiable

Preferred journals

  • JISSN, Nutrients, AJCN, EJSS, IJSNEM, JSCR, MSSE, BJSM, Sports Medicine

Content framing: Professional athlete sharing personal research findings. Always: “the research I found shows…” Not a nutritionist.

Section 02
Repeatable Script Framework
⚠️
Framing note: You are not a nutritionist or doctor — and that's actually a strength, not a weakness. Frame everything as "here's what I use and what the research I found says" rather than prescribing. This is more relatable AND legally safer. The exact framing lines are built into each script template below.
Video 1 — Research Drop
30–45s
0–3s
THE HOOK
"Here's everything you need to know about [SUPPLEMENT] in 30 seconds."
Delivery: Straight to camera, zero preamble. No "hey guys", no intro. The hook IS the first word. Stand slightly off-centre on green screen.
3–8s
THE PROBLEM / MYTH
"Most people [get this wrong / don't know this / are wasting money on this]."
Purpose: Creates the tension that keeps people watching. One sentence max.
8–22s
THE INSIGHT
"The research I've looked at shows [KEY FACT]. As an athlete I [what you personally do with this info]."
Framing tip: "The research shows" or "studies I've read" keeps it as sharing, not prescribing. Show the research article/chart on the green screen behind you at this moment. Don't need to cite it perfectly — just having it visible builds credibility.
22–30s
THE PERSONAL STAMP
"I track mine in SUPPSTACKD — link in bio, free to download."
Key: Don't say "you should use this app." Say "I track mine in…" — personal, not pushy. The CTA naturally follows the insight.
Video 2 — App Demo
30–45s
0–4s
THE PAIN POINT
"If you take supplements but never actually know if they're working — this is for you."
Alternate hooks: "I used to forget half my supplements every day." / "Nobody tells you how to actually track whether your stack is working."
4–10s
THE SETUP
"This is SUPPSTACKD — I built it because nothing else did what I needed as an athlete."
Start screen recording here. Face cam in corner (PiP). Keep face cam on the entire time so it feels personal, not like a product ad.
10–35s
THE DEMO
"So what I do every morning is [SPECIFIC FEATURE WALKTHROUGH]. And the thing that's changed for me is [genuine benefit you've noticed]."
One feature per video. Never try to show everything. Show it in real use — your actual stack, real feel scores, real data. Authenticity converts better than polish.
35–45s
THE CTA
"Free to download — link in bio. Let me know what stack you're running in the comments."
The comment prompt is intentional. "What stack are you running?" generates real replies and boosts engagement velocity on the algorithm.
Video 3 — Lifestyle Loop (No Talking)
8–12s
0–3s
OPENING SHOT
Strong visual hook — close-up of supplement bottles, hands opening a shaker, or your feet hitting the volleyball court.
Rule: The first frame must be visually arresting enough to stop the scroll. No slow pans to open. Start in the middle of the action.
3–8s
THE SEQUENCE
2–3 cuts. Morning pills → training hall → post-session shake. OR: supplement lineup → app open → feel score logged. Tell a silent story.
Cut on the beat. Use a trending sound or a clean lo-fi track. The algorithm weights videos that use trending audio heavily. Check the trending audio tab before filming.
8–12s
END CARD
Text overlay on final frame: "Track your stack → suppstackd.com" or the SUPPSTACKD logo on black.
Loop design: If possible, make the last frame connect back to the first visually — this makes the video loop seamlessly, which massively increases watch time metrics.

Audio rules
1
V1 & V2: Your voice should be the only audio. No background music under talking. It competes with your voice and hurts comprehension.
2
V3: Always use music. Check trending sounds in TikTok/Reels audio library the day you post. Using a trending track in the first 24 hours of it trending = significant algorithm boost.
3
Record in a quiet room. Background noise — even HVAC hum — kills perceived professionalism. Use a lapel mic if possible (the $30 ones on Amazon are fine).
4
Speak slightly faster than feels comfortable. Content moves quickly in short-form. If it feels fast to you, it probably feels right to your viewer.
